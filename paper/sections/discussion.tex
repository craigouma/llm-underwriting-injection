\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Where the LLM sits matters more than what the prompt says.} For the two models that do not approve everyone, an LLM decider is steered by injected instructions in \asrPhiAInstr{}\% (Phi) and \asrOlmoAInstr{}\% (Olmo) of attacked pairs. A prompt-level defense lowers these figures to \asrPhiAdefInstr{}\% and \asrOlmoAdefInstr{}\%, and the result depends on the attack category, the prompt wording and the sampling temperature. Using the same models as extractors for a scorecard gives \asrPhiBInstr{}\% and \asrOlmoBInstr{}\%, and adding checks leaves no success in \asrPhiCInstrn{} Phi pairs or \asrOlmoCInstrn{} Olmo pairs. The architecture removes the channel through which the instruction acts, because the extractor's output has no field for a decision.

\textbf{Instructions that name values still pass through the extractor.} H1 fails because two variants tell the extractor which numbers to write. This is the one route by which an instruction reaches the decision in B. It is closed by the same cross-document checks that handle ordinary falsification, because the injected income contradicts the declared income and the deposits. The design-pattern literature names attacks that change content while leaving the data flow intact as outside what separation protects against~\cite{debenedetti2025camel}. IO3 and DX3 become attacks of that kind once the extractor writes their values into a field.

\textbf{Extraction manipulation is application fraud, and the LLM is not the weak point.} With an oracle extractor, B succeeds as often as with any of the three LLMs (the differences are 0.1, $-3.0$ and 0.0 points). A model that reads documents perfectly passes on a falsified value perfectly. The defenses that work here are those a lender already uses against fraud: corroborate a value in a second source, check units and ranges, and compare with the applicant's peer group. The checks in C lower the category 5 ASR from \asrQwenBExtract{}\%, \asrPhiBExtract{}\% and \asrOlmoBExtract{}\% to \asrQwenCExtract{}\%, \asrPhiCExtract{}\% and \asrOlmoCExtract{}\%. What remains is concentrated where the bundle holds a single source (late payments, revolving balance) and in forgeries that agree across documents. A bureau report for delinquency and balances would remove the first group, and the paper cannot say how much of the second group a real verification process catches.

\textbf{The cost of the checks depends on the extractor.} C flags \reviewQwenC{}\%, \reviewPhiC{}\% and \reviewOlmoC{}\% of clean bundles. In \flagWrongPhi{}\% to \flagWrongQwen{}\% of the flagged bundles the extraction has a wrongly extracted or missing field, such as net pay in place of gross pay for Qwen and empty fields for Olmo. A lender who adopts C should measure its clean review rate on its own documents before deployment. With a more accurate extractor the burden of review falls, and the attack success rate does not rise. The currency and range checks cost almost nothing on clean inputs and remove the shilling payslip (EM14), which succeeds against B in 60.0\% to 92.0\% of cases, so they are the first checks to add.

\textbf{What the comparison does not show.} The deciders in this study are small models given a policy prompt. Qwen approves nearly everyone, and Phi and Olmo approve nearly no one, so their utility as underwriters is poor (AUROC between \aurocOlmoA{} and \aurocPhiA{}) and their ASR on category 5 says little. A larger or fine-tuned decider may do better on both. The finding that holds across the three models is the ordering of the architectures under instruction injection, and it does not depend on how well the decider underwrites.

\textbf{Deviations and surprises.} Two of the preregistered hypotheses held for all or most models (H2 for two of three, H3 for three of three) and one did not (H1). The Phi verdict for H3 flipped from not supported to supported when the group flags of the replay were corrected to the preregistered run sizes (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}). Before the correction the clean review rate of C and the refer rate of B for Phi were equal to the first decimal. The generation grid used more compute than planned (Section~\ref{sec:repro}), and the Olmo-3 7B run covers fewer applicants than the other two.
